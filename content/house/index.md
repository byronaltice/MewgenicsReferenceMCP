The **player's home** is the primary setting for the gameplay of [Mewgenics](/wiki/Mewgenics "Mewgenics") when the player is not adventuring. In this mode, players can place down furniture, tend to their cats, listen to the radio with their obtained sound tracks, store items, and send teams of cats on adventures.

### Furniture Tab

[Furniture](/wiki/Furniture "Furniture") can be placed around the house and grant various effects. It is obtained mainly by buying it from [![NPC Baby Jack Icon.svg](images/NPC_Baby_Jack_Icon.svg)](/wiki/Baby_Jack "Baby Jack") [Baby Jack](/wiki/Baby_Jack "Baby Jack") or from gathering ![ITEM Furniture Box.svg](images/ITEM_Furniture_Box.svg) [Furniture Boxes](/wiki/Furniture_Box "Furniture Box") [![ITEM Furniture Box.svg](images/ITEM_Furniture_Box.svg)](/wiki/Furniture_Box "Furniture Box")  [Furniture Box](/wiki/Furniture_Box "Furniture Box") (Trinket)  +8 [![UI Shield Icon.svg](images/UI_Shield_Icon.svg)](/wiki/Shield "Shield"), -5 [![Speed](images/Stat_Speed.svg)](/wiki/Stats#Speed "Speed")Speed  
Breaks when [![UI Shield Icon.svg](images/UI_Shield_Icon.svg)](/wiki/Shield "Shield") is depleted.  
Becomes a random piece of furniture when you return home.  during adventures. Furniture can increase/decrease your house stats, and each piece has a rare alternate variant with boosted stats and a unique look. So far there are 700+ pieces (not including alternates).

### Examine Cat Mode

This allows the player to see any owned cat's [stats](/wiki/Stats "Stats"), [abilities](/wiki/Abilities "Abilities"), and [afflictions](/wiki/Afflictions "Afflictions"). Donating Kittens to [![NPC Tink Icon.svg](images/NPC_Tink_Icon.svg)](/wiki/Tink "Tink") [Tink](/wiki/Tink "Tink") will allow more stats to be seen, such as sexuality, libido and a cat's relationships with other cats, Clicking a cat's name after Tink's fifth reward opens a UI to apply a symbol to them.

[![Houseexaminecat.png](images/Houseexaminecat.png)](/wiki/File:Houseexaminecat.png)

### Item Storage

After an adventure or getting a item as a reward from [![NPC Frank Icon.svg](images/NPC_Frank_Icon.svg)](/wiki/Frank "Frank") [Frank](/wiki/Frank "Frank"), [![NPC Organ Grinder Icon.svg](images/NPC_Organ_Grinder_Icon.svg)](/wiki/Organ_Grinder "Organ Grinder") [Organ Grinder](/wiki/Organ_Grinder "Organ Grinder") or buying them from [![NPC Tracy Icon.svg](images/NPC_Tracy_Icon.svg)](/wiki/Tracy "Tracy") [Tracy](/wiki/Tracy "Tracy"), items will be placed into the storage. If there is no space, items are automatically placed in the trash. At the end of the day, the trash will be emptied and any items in there will be lost forever. Items in storage can be kept there indefinitely until used or equipped on a cat. More storage slots obtained by fulfilling [![NPC Butch Icon.svg](images/NPC_Butch_Icon.svg)](/wiki/Butch "Butch") [Butch](/wiki/Butch "Butch") cat quotas.

[![Houseitemstorage.png](images/Houseitemstorage.png)](/wiki/File:Houseitemstorage.png)

### Adventure Box

There is a cardboard box on the right side of the house. The box will not be present at night when returning from or being defeated during an adventure. This is where you place the cats you'd like to send on an adventure, up to a maximum of 4. If you've upgraded [![NPC Tracy Icon.svg](images/NPC_Tracy_Icon.svg)](/wiki/Tracy "Tracy") [Tracy](/wiki/Tracy "Tracy") high enough to get any blank collars, a preview of what classes they will be will appear as symbols on the side of the box. These previews will change every day. In the image below, the box shows that the blank collars will be [![Thief Icon.svg](images/Thief_Icon.svg)](/wiki/Thief "Thief") [Thief](/wiki/Thief "Thief"), [![Tank Icon.svg](images/Tank_Icon.svg)](/wiki/Tank "Tank") [Tank](/wiki/Tank "Tank"), and [![Druid Icon.svg](images/Druid_Icon.svg)](/wiki/Druid "Druid") [Druid](/wiki/Druid "Druid").

### Ending Days

By pressing the "End Day" sign above the adventure box, the player is allowed to pass to the next in-game day at the cost of 1 Food per cat. If this cost cannot be paid, Cats that were not fed will start to [starve](/wiki/Food#Food_Deficits "Food"). In the progression to the next day, a multitude of events might happen, such as two cats [breeding](/wiki/Breeding "Breeding") or getting into a [fight](/wiki/Fighting "Fighting") - the latter of which can result in the death of either cat - or nothing at all.

### Stray Cats

Main article: [Stray Cats](/wiki/Stray_Cats "Stray Cats")

Every day, a stray cat will wander up to your house. They can be taken into your home to replace old cats in your adventuring party and diversify the genepool. Ending day with stray still standing by the pipe will cause it to be discarded automatically.

[![Housestraycat.png](images/Housestraycat.png)](/wiki/File:Housestraycat.png)

## Upgrades

[![NPC Frank Icon.svg](images/NPC_Frank_Icon.svg)](/wiki/Frank "Frank") [Frank](/wiki/Frank "Frank") can upgrade the player's house to have more rooms, with the maximum amount of rooms being five. The first room and the first attic have an area of 112 and 30 squares respectively. The upgraded rooms each have an area of 112 squares, while the upgraded attic has an area of 136 squares but does not offer any ceiling Anchor Points.

## Seasons

The game cycles through seasons every 3 in-game months.

- Months have an equal amount of days as real life ones.
- The game features leap years, with the first on year 3.

A new save file starts on June 1st.

Seasons can start weather effects around the house, affecting Chapter 1 when embarking on an [Adventure](/wiki/Adventure "Adventure").

The first and last week of a season blend their weathers together:

- Last week of Fall & First week of Winter can be either Windy or Snowy.

## Trivia

- In the original prototype of Mewgenics, the House had a bigger role and served as the overall primary gameplay space as the concept of adventuring didn't exist.
- Based on peeks at the game's code:
  - The upgrade progression may have been less about adding space and more about *compartmentalizing* at one point, with upgrade names suggesting that the 2nd and 4th regular rooms would have come from splitting the (initially larger) 1st and 3rd regular rooms in half.
  - Five **Basement** upgrades were going to be available from the start of the game, as low-ceiling equivalents of the upgraded attic, but these currently go [unused](/wiki/Unused_Content "Unused Content").
    - Based on dev comments, they would've resembled the 5 main floors from "The Binding of Isaac".
